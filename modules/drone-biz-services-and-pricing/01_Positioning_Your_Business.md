# Positioning Your Business

How you position your business determines how customers perceive you. Are you the budget option? The premium provider? The specialist who offers something nobody else does? This decision affects everything from your pricing to your marketing.

## Choosing Your Position

You generally have three positioning options: low-price solution (competing on affordability), mid-market (balancing quality and price), or premium/high-end (commanding top prices for exceptional quality or unique offerings).

If you have the skills and a high-quality product, do not be afraid to position yourself as premium. Undercutting the market signals lower quality, and it makes it much harder to raise prices later.

## Your Positioning Statement

A positioning statement is a concise description of who you serve, what you offer, and what makes you different. Here is a template:

**For** [target market] **who need** [customer need], **our** [product/service] **offers** [key benefit]. **Unlike** [competition], **we** [key differentiator].

**Example:** "For commercial realtors who need high-quality aerial photography, our Gold Package offers both aerial and ground photography with same-day image delivery. Unlike competitors who require week-long turnaround times, we deliver polished images within 24 hours."

This becomes the guiding philosophy for your business. Everything from your website copy to your sales conversations should align with this positioning.

## Key Takeaways

- Your positioning determines customer perception and pricing power
- Do not default to being the cheapest option — it signals lower quality
- Craft a clear positioning statement that guides all your business decisions
- Align everything — website, marketing, conversations — with your chosen position
