# Pricing Methods and Strategies

Pricing is one of the most critical decisions you will make. Too low and you will not cover your costs. Too high and nobody will buy. The right price covers your expenses, is acceptable to your customers, and is in line with market rates.

## Three Methods of Pricing

### Market-Based Pricing

Look at what your competitors charge and set your prices relative to theirs. Price higher if your product is superior or you offer more value. Price lower if you are new and building a client base — but be careful not to undervalue your work.

### Value-Based Pricing

Determine your price based on the value you deliver to the customer, not just your costs. For example, if your drone inspection saves a roofing company two hours of work (worth $50/hour to them), you could charge $60 for that service — they save time and you make money. If your footage will appear in a national TV commercial versus a local ad, the value is dramatically different and so should the price be.

### Cost-Plus Pricing

Calculate all your costs for delivering a service, then add a markup percentage. This requires understanding every expense: your time (at an hourly rate you set for yourself), equipment wear and tear, travel costs (fuel, vehicle maintenance), software subscriptions, insurance (prorated per job), and an overhead percentage for rent, utilities, and administrative costs.

**Example calculation:**

| Cost Component | Amount |
|---------------|--------|
| Your time (10 hours × $75/hr) | $750 |
| Equipment wear and tear | $50 |
| Travel (fuel, vehicle) | $20 |
| Subtotal | $820 |
| + 10% overhead markup | $82 |
| **Total price to customer** | **$902** |

> **Warning:** If cost-plus pricing pushes your prices above what the market will bear, it may indicate your overhead is too high. You may need to streamline your operation rather than simply raising prices.

## Pricing Strategies

Beyond the method you use to calculate prices, consider these psychological strategies:

**Penetration pricing** — Start with lower prices to win market share and build a client base, then gradually raise prices as your reputation grows and clients appreciate your quality.

**Premium pricing** — Set high prices from the start to signal high quality. This works well if you have exceptional skills and a strong portfolio.

**Psychological pricing** — Price at $995 instead of $1,000, or $295 instead of $300. It sounds trivial, but research consistently shows it influences buying decisions.

## Common Pricing Mistakes

**Do not be the lowest price.** Lowest price is almost always associated with lowest quality in customers' minds. **Do not underestimate your costs.** Factor in everything — fuel, wear and tear, software, insurance, licensing, electricity, rent. Small costs add up fast and will eat your profit if you ignore them. **Do not forget the value of your time.** If you spend three hours on flight prep and data processing for every hour of flying, your pricing must reflect all of that time.

## Key Takeaways

- Choose a pricing method (market-based, value-based, or cost-plus) that fits your business
- Never underestimate your true costs — include every expense, no matter how small
- Avoid being the cheapest option; it hurts your brand perception
- Use psychological pricing strategies to influence buying decisions
- Revisit and adjust your pricing as you gain experience and market data
