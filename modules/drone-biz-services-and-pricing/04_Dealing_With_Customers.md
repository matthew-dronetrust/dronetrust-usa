# How to Deal With Customers

Customer management is an art that directly impacts your profitability and sanity. You need clear policies for handling different scenarios before they arise.

## Customers Who Want Less Than Your Minimum

Some clients will say they only need "one quick photo" and want to pay accordingly. Be careful accepting very small jobs. You still have to drive to the location, set up your equipment, plan the flight, get any required authorizations, fly, process the data, and deliver the result. All of that takes time regardless of how simple the job seems.

Have a minimum engagement fee or minimum booking time to protect your profitability. Some operators set a four-hour or eight-hour minimum for this reason.

## Customers With Low Budgets

Inevitably, some potential clients will want your services but claim they cannot afford your prices. You have options: you can negotiate a reduced scope to fit their budget, you can take the job at a discount as an investment in future business (if you genuinely believe it will lead to more work), or you can politely decline.

If you do offer a discount, always show the client the full value of what they received. Put the regular price on the invoice with the discount clearly noted. This way, they understand the true value of your work and will not expect the discounted price to be permanent.

> **Warning:** "This will lead to a lot more projects in the future" is the oldest trick in the book. Do not rely on vague promises of future work. If you discount, do it for your own strategic reasons, not because someone else promised you something they may never deliver.

## The Exposure Trap

Someone will eventually offer you "exposure" instead of payment. Exposure does not pay bills. When was the last time you watched aerial footage and checked who filmed it? Unless the exposure comes with a concrete, measurable benefit — and it almost never does — politely decline.

Good and fast will not be cheap. Good and cheap will not be fast. Fast and cheap will not be good. Setting this expectation early prevents misunderstandings and protects your reputation.

## Key Takeaways

- Set minimum engagement fees to protect against unprofitable small jobs
- If you offer discounts, always show the full value on the invoice
- Do not accept "exposure" as payment — it rarely delivers real value
- Educate customers about the fast-cheap-good tradeoff upfront
- Have clear policies before situations arise, not after
