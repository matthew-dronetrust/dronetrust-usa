# Finding and Managing Customers

Without customers, you have no business. Finding them requires consistent effort, and keeping them requires excellent service and relationship management.

## Finding Customers In Person

Personal connections remain one of the most effective ways to generate business. Introduce yourself to potential clients, explain what you do and how you solve their problems, and always have business cards ready. Join your local Chamber of Commerce, attend trade shows related to your target industry, and visit potential clients directly — if you do construction site monitoring, visit active construction sites and introduce yourself to project managers.

## Your Elevator Speech

Have a 30-second pitch ready at all times. It should cover who you are, what you do, and how you can help solve the listener's problem. Practice it until it feels natural, not rehearsed.

## Run Free Events

Host events that showcase drone capabilities in your community. Educational events build goodwill and position you as the local expert. When you are flying publicly, answer people's questions and show them what drones can do.

## Keeping Your Customers

Retaining customers is significantly cheaper than acquiring new ones. Deliver what you promised, on time. Re-engage past customers periodically — a quick check-in email asking if they need anything can generate repeat business. Notify existing customers when you add new services.

## Managing Customer Expectations

Do not take on more work than you can handle — overcommitting leads to missed deadlines and unhappy clients. Listen carefully to what customers want, but also know when to say no. Get clear instructions before starting any project to avoid costly do-overs. If you are running behind schedule, communicate proactively — clients are far more understanding when you tell them in advance rather than missing a deadline silently.

> **Tip:** Not every customer is worth keeping. If a client is consistently difficult, demanding work beyond the agreed scope, or refusing to pay fairly, it is okay to part ways professionally.

## Key Takeaways

- Personal connections and networking are your most effective lead generation tools
- Have your elevator speech ready at all times
- Customer retention is cheaper than acquisition — nurture existing relationships
- Communicate proactively about delays or issues
- Know when to say no to unprofitable or problematic clients
