# Managing Finances, ROI, and Making Decisions

Day-to-day financial management and sound decision-making are what separate businesses that survive from those that fail.

## Managing Your Income

Never let accounts receivable age without action. If your payment terms say 30 days, follow up on day 31. Have a clear escalation process: a friendly reminder first, a formal notice second, and legal action (small claims court) as a last resort. Before pursuing legal action, weigh the amount owed against the time and cost of collection.

## Managing Your Expenses

Ask yourself constantly: is your business lean enough? The two biggest expense traps for growing businesses are employees (salary, taxes, benefits, PTO) and office space. Before hiring, ask whether you truly need another person or whether you can handle the workload with better systems. Before renting an office, ask whether you actually need one or whether working from home is sufficient.

> **Warning:** Always keep your eye on cash flow. A profitable business on paper can still fail if cash is not available when bills are due.

## Return on Investment (ROI)

Every significant purchase or investment should have a positive return. Before buying a $10,000 drone, calculate how many jobs it would take to pay it off at your current pricing. Before spending $500 on advertising, track whether it generates more than $500 in revenue. ROI is not always immediate — a marketing campaign might take months to generate leads — but you must be tracking it to know whether your investments are paying off.

## Making Tough Decisions

Running a business means making decisions that are not always popular. You will need to decide when to hire (when you are so busy with non-revenue tasks that you cannot take on more clients), when to upgrade equipment (when your current gear limits the jobs you can accept), when to raise prices (when your costs increase or your value justifies it), and when to fire a client (when the relationship costs more in time and stress than it generates in revenue).

Embrace trial and error. Not every decision will be correct. Failure is part of the process — the key is to learn from mistakes quickly and adjust.

## Key Takeaways

- Enforce payment terms consistently — aging receivables kill cash flow
- Keep your business lean, especially regarding employees and office space
- Calculate ROI on every significant investment
- Make decisions based on data, not emotion
- Expect trial and error — learn quickly and adapt
