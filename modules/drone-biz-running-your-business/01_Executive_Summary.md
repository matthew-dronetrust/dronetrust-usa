# Writing Your Executive Summary

The executive summary is the first section of your business plan but the last one you write. It summarizes everything you have developed throughout the planning process.

## Purpose

The executive summary is what investors, bankers, or partners will read first — and possibly the only section they read. It must be compelling enough to make them want to continue reading. Even if you are not seeking investment, writing the executive summary forces you to distill your entire business into its essence.

## What to Include

Keep it to one page maximum. Cover your company description and mission, the problem you solve and your solution, your target market and competitive advantage, your service offerings, your marketing strategy highlights, your team, and your financial projections summary.

> **Tip:** Think of the executive summary as your elevator pitch on paper. It should answer: What do you do? Who do you do it for? Why are you different? And will it make money?

## Writing Tips

Make it well-written and professional — this is your first impression. Focus on what makes you different from the competition. Be specific rather than vague. Lead with the most compelling aspects of your business. If you are seeking funding, clearly state how much you need and how it will be used.

## Assignment 25

Write your executive summary. This is the final assignment in your business plan.

## Key Takeaways

- The executive summary is written last but appears first in your business plan
- Keep it to one page — concise, compelling, and specific
- Focus on your unique value proposition and financial viability
- This is your business pitch on paper — make it count
