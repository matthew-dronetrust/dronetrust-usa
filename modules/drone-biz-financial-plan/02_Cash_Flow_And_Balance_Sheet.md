# Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

Understanding the difference between profitability on paper and actual cash in your bank account is critical. Many profitable businesses have failed because they ran out of cash.

## P&L vs. Cash Flow

Your P&L shows whether your business is profitable in theory. Your cash flow shows whether you actually have money available to pay your bills right now. You can show a profit on paper but be completely broke if your customers have not paid you yet.

> **Danger:** Without a positive cash flow, your company cannot survive. This is the single most important financial concept for any business owner.

Consider this scenario: you complete $10,000 worth of work in January, but your clients have 60-day payment terms. Your P&L shows $10,000 in revenue, but your bank account has $0 from those jobs until March. Meanwhile, you still need to pay rent, insurance, fuel, and your own salary.

## Cash Flow Statement Structure

Your cash flow statement has three simple components: **Cash on Hand** (money available from previous months), **Cash In** (revenue actually received this period), and **Cash Out** (expenses actually paid this period).

Cash on hand plus cash in minus cash out equals your ending cash position. If that number is negative, you have a problem that requires immediate action — borrowing money, using credit, or cutting expenses.

## Using Cash Flow Wisely

Your cash flow projection helps you anticipate problems before they happen. If you can see that March will be a tight month, you can prepare by building reserves in January and February, adjusting payment terms with clients, or arranging a line of credit in advance.

## Balance Sheet Overview

A balance sheet shows a snapshot of your financial position at a point in time. It has three components: **Assets** (what you own — cash, equipment, accounts receivable), **Liabilities** (what you owe — loans, accounts payable, taxes due), and **Owner's Equity** (retained earnings and dividends).

Assets must always equal liabilities plus owner's equity — that is why it is called a balance sheet. For most small drone businesses, the P&L and cash flow are more immediately useful for day-to-day decisions.

## Key Takeaways

- Cash flow is king — profitable businesses can fail without positive cash flow
- Always know when you will actually receive payment, not just when you earn it
- Use cash flow projections to anticipate and prepare for tight months
- The balance sheet is useful but less critical for daily decision-making than P&L and cash flow
