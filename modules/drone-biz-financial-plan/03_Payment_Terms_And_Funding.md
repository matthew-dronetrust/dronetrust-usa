# Payment Terms and Getting Funds

Your payment terms directly affect your cash flow, and understanding your funding options can make or break your business in its early stages.

## Setting Payment Terms

Decide how long customers have to pay: 15 days, 30 days, or 60 days. The longer you allow, the more your cash flow suffers. Clearly communicate your terms before starting any work. Also decide what happens when a client does not pay on time — will you charge a late fee? Send to collections? Take legal action through small claims court?

Consider offering a small cash discount (such as 2%) for immediate payment. Since credit card processing fees are typically around 3%, a customer paying cash at a 2% discount still saves you 1% compared to a credit card transaction.

> **Tip:** Build credit card processing fees into your prices rather than charging customers extra for paying by card. Price everything assuming a 3% fee, and if someone pays cash, you come out slightly ahead.

## Funding Your Business

If you need external funding, here are your options:

**Bank loans** require a business plan. Some banks specialize in small business lending. SBA (Small Business Administration) loans are government-backed options with favorable terms for qualifying businesses.

**Venture capital and angel investors** provide funding in exchange for equity in your company. Be extremely careful about what you give away. Giving up 5% of your company for $3,000 is almost never a good deal — if you cannot save $3,000 on your own, you may not be ready to start a business.

> **Warning:** Friends and family loans are the riskiest funding source for your relationships. If you go this route, put everything in writing and make sure they understand they may lose their investment. Most advisors recommend against this approach entirely.

## Exit Strategy

If you are seeking investment, investors will want to know your exit strategy. How will the business eventually be sold or transferred? Even if you plan to run it indefinitely, think about how the business would be valued and transitioned.

## Use of Funds

If seeking investment, clearly document exactly how you will spend the money — equipment purchases, marketing budget, operating expenses for the first six months, etc. Investors want to see a specific, thoughtful plan for their money.

## Key Takeaways

- Clear payment terms protect your cash flow — communicate them before starting work
- Build processing fees into your prices rather than surprising customers with surcharges
- Be very cautious about giving up equity for small amounts of funding
- Avoid borrowing from friends and family if possible
- Document exactly how any investment funds will be used
