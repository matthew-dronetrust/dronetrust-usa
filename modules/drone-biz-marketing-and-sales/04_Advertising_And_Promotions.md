# Advertising, Promotions, and Metrics

Advertising puts your business in front of potential customers, but it costs money. The key is ensuring your spend generates more revenue than it costs.

## Online Advertising

**Search engine ads** (Google Ads) display your business when people search for relevant keywords. You pay per click, so monitor conversion rates closely. Set daily budget limits to control spending.

**Social media ads** (Facebook, Instagram) let you target specific demographics, interests, and locations. Always include a call to action and track your return on investment.

> **Tip:** If you spend $150 on ads and land a $1,000 contract, you are $850 ahead. If you spend $800 for that same contract, evaluate whether the remaining $200 profit is worth it.

## Promotions and Discounts

Strategic promotions bring in new customers. Consider introductory pricing for first-time clients, seasonal discounts during slow periods, package deals for bulk purchases, and referral incentives. Think of promotions like a discovery flight — you might break even initially, but if the customer becomes a long-term client, the payoff is enormous.

## Milestones and Metrics

Set clear milestones and track key metrics: number of new customers, monthly revenue, customer retention rate, advertising ROI, and average job value. If you planned for 10 clients in year one and have only 3 after six months, adjust your strategy.

## Assumptions

Every business plan rests on assumptions — how many clients you will win, how much they will spend, how often they will return. Document these clearly and be conservative. The more assumptions your plan relies on, the riskier it is.

- Track the return on every advertising dollar
- Strategic promotions can drive new business when designed thoughtfully
