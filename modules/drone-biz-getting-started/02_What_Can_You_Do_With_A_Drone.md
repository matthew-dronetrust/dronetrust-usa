# What Can You Do With a Drone?

One of the most common questions from aspiring drone business owners is simply: "What can I actually do with a drone?" The answer is broad and growing every day, but it is important to understand both the opportunities and the reality behind them.

## Common Drone Business Opportunities

There are many established and emerging markets for drone services:

- **Real Estate Photography and Videography** — Aerial photos and videos for residential and commercial property listings. This is one of the most popular entry points into the drone business.
- **General Photography and Videography** — Events, commercials, promotional videos, and creative content.
- **Mapping and Surveying** — Orthomosaic maps, 3D models, and topographic data. Note that calling yourself a "surveyor" requires proper certification — do not use that title without the appropriate license.
- **Agriculture** — Plant health assessment (NDVI), crop monitoring, and even seed planting.
- **Inspections** — Roofs, power lines, solar panels, wind turbines, cell towers, and industrial facilities.
- **Public Safety** — Firefighting support, law enforcement, and search and rescue operations.
- **Construction** — Progress monitoring, site surveys, and volumetric measurements.
- **Transportation and Delivery** — A rapidly growing sector, though currently dominated by large companies.

> **Tip:** This list is not exhaustive. New industries are adopting drone technology every day. Keep an eye on emerging use cases that match your skills.

## The Drone Is Often Just a Data Collection Tool

Here is something critical to understand: in many drone applications, the drone itself is just a tool for gathering data. Once you have captured aerial imagery, LiDAR data, or thermal scans, the real work often begins on the ground — processing, analyzing, and presenting that data to your client.

This means your value as a business is not just your ability to fly. It is your ability to deliver a complete, actionable product. A farmer does not just want photos of their field — they want a plant health analysis. A roofer does not just want aerial images — they want an inspection report.

## Your Job Description Is Bigger Than You Think

When you run a drone business, flying is only a small portion of what you do. Here is a realistic look at your daily responsibilities: finding new clients and writing quotes, creating and executing marketing campaigns, preparing for flights (site research, airspace authorization, waivers), flying the drone and capturing data, editing photos and video or analyzing inspection data, reporting results to customers, managing customer relationships and expectations, reviewing financial documents and paying bills, keeping up with new technology and changing regulations, and managing employees if you grow your team.

> **Warning:** If you are getting into this business thinking you will fly your drone all day, every day — that is not what will happen. The majority of your time will be spent running the business.

## Key Takeaways

- Drone businesses span real estate, mapping, inspections, agriculture, public safety, and more
- The drone is often just the data collection tool — the real value is in processing and delivering results
- Running a drone business means wearing many hats beyond just piloting
- New opportunities are emerging constantly as more industries adopt drone technology
