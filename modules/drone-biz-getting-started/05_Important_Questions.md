# Important Questions to Ask Yourself

Before you start building a business plan, you need to do some serious brainstorming. These questions will help you clarify your direction and identify potential challenges early — long before you invest significant time and money.

## Questions About Your Skills and Strengths

**What are you good at?** This is the foundation of your business. Your clients want an expert, and you are most likely to succeed doing something you already excel at. If people consistently compliment your aerial photography, that is a strong signal. If you have a background in construction, inspections might be your niche.

**Do you have relevant expertise?** Remember that just because you see someone else succeeding with drones does not mean you will automatically succeed the same way. You do not know their background or the skills that got them there.

**What are your strengths and weaknesses?** Be honest with yourself. If you are weak in an area that is critical to your business, you will either need to improve that skill or find someone who complements you.

> **Tip:** If you want to get into a specific niche but lack experience — like power line inspection — invest in getting that expertise first. A power line inspector needs to understand how power lines work, not just how to fly near them.

## Questions About Your Readiness

**How much time can you dedicate?** Building a business requires consistent effort over months and years. If you have a full-time job, think about how many hours per week you can realistically invest. Be honest — this affects everything from your business plan to your timeline.

**Is your family on board?** Starting a business consumes significant time and energy. If you have a partner or family, make sure they understand and support what you are committing to. Nothing undermines a new business faster than resentment at home.

**How much capital can you risk?** The key word here is "risk." Whatever money you put into this business, you may lose. Be clear about what you can afford to invest without putting your financial security at risk.

Consider the startup costs you will face:

| Category | Typical Range |
|----------|--------------|
| Part 107 training and testing | $150 – $400 |
| Drone equipment | $1,000 – $10,000+ |
| Insurance (annual) | $500 – $2,000 |
| Business formation (LLC) | $50 – $500 |
| Website and marketing | $500 – $5,000 |
| Software and tools | $200 – $1,000/year |

## Questions About Your Market

**Is there demand for your skills?** Research whether people in your area (or nationally) are actively looking for the services you want to offer. Are there already many established providers, or is there a gap you can fill?

**Is there already significant competition?** We will do a deep competitive analysis later, but start thinking about who else is operating in your space and what they offer.

**Is there room for growth?** Think about whether your market is expanding or saturated. Can you scale beyond your immediate area if needed?

**Are you willing to travel?** In smaller markets, you may need to travel to find enough clients. Factor in travel time and expenses when evaluating your business model.

## Questions About Your Business Direction

**What will you specialize in?** One of the biggest mistakes new drone business owners make is trying to offer everything. If your website lists 15 different services, clients are unlikely to see you as an expert in any of them. Pick one or two areas where you truly excel, then expand over time.

**What equipment do you have?** The best drone is the one you have right now. Do not fall into "equipment fever" — start making money with your current gear and upgrade when your revenue justifies the investment.

> **Warning:** Do not spend $50,000 on a high-end drone before you have validated that you can earn that money back. We will cover return on investment throughout the course.

**Who are your business mentors?** Think about successful business people you admire. What traits do they have that you can learn from? What strategies have made them successful?

## Key Takeaways

- Honest self-assessment of your skills, time, and capital is essential before starting
- Specialize rather than trying to offer every possible service
- Make sure your family is supportive and your risk tolerance is clear
- Research demand and competition in your target market before investing
- Start with the equipment you have — upgrade when revenue justifies it
