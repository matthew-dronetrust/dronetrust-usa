# First Contact and Discovery

The minute a prospect reaches out is the minute the entire marketing funnel either pays off or breaks down. The single biggest source of lost revenue for drone businesses is not bad ads or weak content — it is mishandled first contact. The good news: a few simple habits dramatically increase your conversion rate, and none of them require talent.

## Speed Wins

The first response is the most important one you will send. Inquiries cool quickly — most studies put the half-life of a B2B lead at under an hour. A response within 30 minutes converts dramatically better than a response within four hours, even if the four-hour reply is more polished.

> **Important:** A short, prompt acknowledgment beats a perfect response written hours later. Reply within an hour even if the full proposal will take a day.

A good acknowledgment looks like: *"Got your message — looking at the date now and will reply with options later today."* That is one sentence and it converts inquiries far better than silence.

## Set the Tone in the First Reply

Your first message should do three things:

- **Confirm you read the inquiry** — name the project, the date, the location
- **Show competence** — one specific, useful sentence about how you would approach it
- **Make the next step obvious** — a clear question or call to action

Compare two openers:

> *"Yes, I do drone work. Let me know if you have any questions."*

vs.

> *"Thanks for reaching out about the Lakeside listing on May 14 — beautiful property. For a luxury home like that, I would plan a 30-minute aerial session right around golden hour to get the lake reflection right. Are you open to a quick 10-minute call this week so I can scope the deliverables?"*

The second positions you as a professional, names the project, signals expertise, and proposes a tiny, low-friction next step. It is also barely longer than the first.

## The Discovery Conversation

Most drone operators jump straight from inquiry to quote, skipping discovery entirely. That is a mistake. A 10–20 minute discovery call (or thoughtful email exchange) almost always:

- Reveals the real outcome the customer cares about
- Surfaces deliverables the customer has not asked for but would value
- Identifies budget reality before you draft a quote
- Lets you raise red flags early — bad sites, unrealistic timelines, scope creep

Discovery is where you stop being "another drone guy quoting" and start being "the operator who actually understood my project."

> **Tip:** Pricing without discovery is gambling. Every quote you send blind is a guess at what the customer wants and what they will pay.

Talk less than 30% of the time. Take notes. End every call with an explicit next step ("I will send a written quote by Thursday morning — you said end of day Friday works for review?"). Vague endings are where deals die.

## Channels and Etiquette

Match the customer's preferred channel. If they emailed, reply by email. If they DM'd, DM back. If they called, call back. Forcing your favoured channel ("can we hop on a Zoom?") on a customer who clearly prefers text is a small but real friction. The exception: discovery itself almost always works better as a short call than as ping-pong email — but ask, never assume.

> **Warning:** Avoid response-time anti-patterns: replying after multiple days, over-apologising for delays, or sending a wall of text instead of a quick acknowledgment. None of these inspire confidence.

## Key Takeaways

- Speed of first response is the highest-leverage variable in lead conversion
- A great first reply confirms the project, shows competence, and proposes a low-friction next step
- A 15–20 minute discovery conversation is the difference between guessing and quoting
- Match the customer's preferred channel; nudge to a call only when it adds clear value
- End every conversation with an explicit, dated next step
