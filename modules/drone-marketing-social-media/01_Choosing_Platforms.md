# Choosing the Right Platforms

Social media is the most over-prescribed marketing channel in the drone industry. Every blog says "post on every platform," and every solo operator who tries it burns out in three months. The right answer is the opposite — pick fewer platforms, post more deliberately, and ignore everything else.

## Match the Platform to the Customer

The right platform is the one your *target customer* uses, not the one *you* enjoy. A wedding-focused drone operator should probably be on Instagram. A drone operator targeting general contractors and engineering firms should probably be on LinkedIn. A drone operator targeting tourism boards and destinations should probably be on TikTok and YouTube. Pick based on where your buyer already scrolls.

> **Important:** "I get the most likes on Instagram" is not a good reason to use Instagram if your buyers live on LinkedIn. Likes from other drone pilots do not pay invoices.

## Platform Strengths in 2026

A short, honest field guide:

- **Instagram** — The default for visual storytelling. Strong for real estate, weddings, lifestyle, tourism, and consumer work. Reels are the engine; static posts barely move.
- **YouTube** — The strongest long-term asset because videos rank in Google search for years. Best for tutorials, case studies, behind-the-scenes, and longer cinematic showcases.
- **LinkedIn** — Underused by drone operators and increasingly important. Best for commercial, industrial, inspections, mapping, and any B2B niche.
- **TikTok** — The fastest path to reach for vertical, dramatic, or surprising aerial work. Great for top-of-funnel discovery; weaker for direct B2B conversion.
- **Facebook** — Aging demographically but still essential for local community work, weddings in some regions, and for joining buyer-side groups (realtor associations, community boards).

> **Tip:** Each platform punishes inactivity and rewards consistency. Two well-run profiles beat five abandoned ones, every time.

## The "Two-Platform Rule"

Pick two platforms — one for **discovery** and one for **trust** — and ignore the rest for at least the first six months.

The discovery platform's job is to put you in front of people who do not yet know you exist. The trust platform's job is to deepen the relationship — let prospects watch a longer piece, read a case study, and decide you are the right hire.

## When to Add a Third Platform

Add a third platform only when both your first two are running on autopilot — meaning consistent posts, real inquiries, and metrics you understand. If either of the first two is still wobbly, fix it before splitting your attention. Most drone operators never need more than two.

## Setting Up Profiles That Convert

Whichever platforms you choose, your profile must do three things in three seconds:

- **Tell visitors who you serve** — "Drone studio for Maine real estate"
- **Show one strong piece of work** — pinned post or featured reel
- **Make it obvious how to hire you** — link in bio, contact button, calendar link

Most drone operators get the first two right and forget the third. A profile that earns attention but has no clear path to inquiry is a leak in your funnel.

> **Warning:** Avoid profile bios full of emojis, slogans, or vague phrases like "creative storyteller." A prospect should know exactly what you do and how to hire you in one glance.

## Assignment 9

Audit every social profile your business currently has. For each, write down: who the audience actually is, whether you posted in the last 14 days, and whether a prospect could find your contact path in 5 seconds. Decide which two profiles you will keep active and put the rest on hold.

## Key Takeaways

- The right platforms are the ones your customers use — not the ones you enjoy
- Pick two platforms: one for discovery, one for trust. Ignore the rest for the first six months.
- Every profile should answer three questions in three seconds: who you serve, proof you are good, how to hire you
- Add a third platform only when the first two are running smoothly
