# Posting Cadence and Tools

Most drone operators fail at social media for a single reason — they cannot keep up the pace they originally promised themselves. The fix is a sustainable cadence supported by simple tools, executed in batches. The goal is "still posting in twelve months," not "viral by Friday."

## How Often You Should Actually Post

Post often enough to stay in front of customers, but not so often that you burn out and disappear. A realistic cadence for a solo drone operator is:

- **Two posts per week on your discovery platform** — usually one reel/short and one carousel or longer-form piece
- **One post every two weeks on your trust platform** — usually a longer case study or video
- **One story or short update per workday** when you are actively shooting — no more

That is roughly three to five planned posts per week, plus light real-time stories. It is enough to compound and infrequent enough to stay sane.

> **Warning:** Daily posting sounds impressive but rarely sustains for more than a few months. The real currency is *consistency over time*, not bursts.

## The Batch Workflow

Trying to post in real time is the fastest way to fall behind. A better approach is batching — produce, schedule, and publish in clearly separated steps:

## Tools Worth Using

You do not need a complicated stack. Three categories cover most needs:

- **Scheduling** — Buffer, Later, or Metricool. Pick one and stop comparing. Free tiers are usually enough for a solo operator.
- **Editing** — CapCut for short-form, DaVinci Resolve or Premiere for longer-form. Build template projects you can reuse so each new edit starts from a known good baseline.
- **Asset management** — One organised folder structure on Google Drive or Dropbox: `clients/[client-name]/[date]/raw` and `clients/[client-name]/[date]/social`. The day you cannot find a clip from a six-month-old shoot is the day you start losing money.

> **Tip:** Always export a 9:16 vertical version *and* a 1:1 square version of every social cut. You will use both more often than you expect.

## Owning the Calendar

A simple content calendar keeps the cadence honest. A spreadsheet with five columns is plenty:

- Date
- Platform
- Pillar (work / expertise / story / proof)
- Caption draft
- Status (idea → draft → scheduled → posted)

Plan two weeks ahead at minimum. The mental relief of knowing what is going out tomorrow is worth far more than the time it costs to plan.

## Don't Optimise for the Algorithm — Optimise for the Buyer

Trends, hashtags, and "best time to post" guides change constantly and matter less than operators think. Two factors actually move outcomes for drone businesses: **show up consistently** and **post content your real buyer would value**. Everything else is rounding error.

> **Important:** Spend five minutes researching trends, then go back to executing your plan. Operators who chase every algorithm change never compound; operators who post twice a week for two years do.

## Key Takeaways

- A sustainable cadence is two posts per week on discovery, one per fortnight on trust — plus light stories
- Batch the work: capture, edit, caption, schedule. Never post in real time as your default mode.
- Simple tooling beats complicated stacks — pick one scheduler, one editor, one folder structure
- A two-week content calendar removes the daily "what do I post?" panic
- Consistency over years compounds; chasing the algorithm does not
