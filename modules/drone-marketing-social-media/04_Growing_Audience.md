# Growing and Engaging Your Audience

Posting alone is half the work. The other half is the steady, slightly tedious habit of *engaging* — replying, commenting on others' work, and building relationships in the spaces where your buyers already gather. Done thoughtfully, engagement compounds faster than reach.

## The 70/20/10 Engagement Split

A useful weekly split for time spent on social:

- **70% creating** — your own posts, edits, captions
- **20% engaging in your buyers' world** — commenting on prospects' posts, joining conversations in industry groups, supporting peer creators
- **10% replying to your own audience** — comments and DMs on your own posts

Most drone operators only do the 70%. Adding the 20% is what produces inquiries from people who would never have found you through your own content alone.

> **Important:** The 20% is the highest-ROI marketing activity available to a solo drone operator. A thoughtful comment on a roofing contractor's post can land a $4,000 contract. A pretty post into the void rarely does.

## Engaging Where Buyers Live

Spend time *in* your customers' world, not just adjacent to it. For each target customer type, build a short list of:

- 10–20 prospect accounts to engage with regularly (real prospects, not other drone pilots)
- 3–5 industry groups or hashtags your prospects follow
- 1–2 association pages, conferences, or influencers your customers respect

Show up there with helpful, on-topic comments — never copy-paste pitches. Done weekly, this builds you into the periphery of your prospect's online life. By the time you DM them, you are not a stranger.

Example: *"Saw your renovation post on the Highview project — that brick work is incredible. I work with contractors like you on quick aerial documentation for marketing and progress reports. If you ever want a sample reel of similar work, happy to send one over — just say the word."*

That is specific, generous, and easy to respond to. Compare it to "Hi, do you need drone services?" — which gets ignored 99 times out of 100.

## Quality Over Quantity in Followers

Followers do not pay invoices; clients do. A focused account with 800 real-prospect followers is worth more than a generic account with 80,000 followers from giveaways or contests. Three signs your audience is the right one:

- The accounts that engage with your posts look like your target customer
- DMs and inquiries come from real businesses, not other drone pilots
- Profile visits convert to inquiries at a rate you can measure

If your audience is mostly other drone enthusiasts, your content is too inwardly focused. Shift posts toward your buyer's world and the audience composition will shift.

> **Warning:** Avoid paid follower growth, follow-for-follow loops, and engagement pods. They poison your audience with people who will never hire you and the algorithm increasingly punishes accounts that look inflated.

## Responding to Comments and DMs

Reply to *everything* — at least for the first 18 months. Every comment is a chance to extend a thread, every DM a chance to start a relationship. Aim to respond within 24 hours, faster on inquiries. Speed of response is one of the most underrated competitive advantages in the drone industry.

> **Tip:** When an inquiry lands in DMs, acknowledge it within the hour even if a fuller reply has to wait. "Got your message — looking at the dates now, will reply with options today." That single message converts more leads than the eventual proposal does.

## Assignment 12

Build a list of 15 target prospect accounts to engage with regularly. Spend 15 minutes a day for two weeks leaving thoughtful, on-topic comments on their posts (no pitching). Track how many of those accounts engage back, follow you, or open a DM thread. Send three concrete-not-spammy DMs at the end of week two using the three-step pattern.

## Key Takeaways

- Split your social time roughly 70% creating, 20% engaging in buyer-land, 10% replying to your own audience
- The 20% in buyers' worlds is the highest-ROI activity available to a solo operator
- DMs work when they are specific, generous, and low-friction — not when they pitch
- Quality of followers matters more than quantity; your audience composition reveals whether your content is buyer-aligned
- Respond fast — within 24 hours always, within an hour for inquiries
