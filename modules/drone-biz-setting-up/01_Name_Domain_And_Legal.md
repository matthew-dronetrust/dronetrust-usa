# Naming, Domain, and Legalizing Your Business

Now that your business plan is complete, it is time to start making things official. This lesson walks you through the practical steps of establishing your business identity and legal entity.

## Naming Your Business

Your business name matters more than you might think. Research trademarks at the USPTO website and check your state's Secretary of State database to ensure no one else is using your chosen name. A few naming tips: say it out loud several times and share it with friends to gauge reactions, check that it does not form unintended words when written without spaces (important for hashtags and URLs), choose something with meaning that communicates what you do, and avoid names that limit future growth — "Phantom Photography" becomes awkward when you switch to a different drone brand.

Search for your potential name on Google, social media platforms, and domain registrars before committing. Tools like Shopify's Business Name Generator can help spark ideas.

## Securing Your Domain Name

Buy your domain name before you finalize your business registration — you do not want someone else to grab it. Keep the domain as short and memorable as possible, and strongly prefer a .com extension for credibility. If your ideal domain is taken but parked (not actively used), you may be able to purchase it from the current owner.

Use GoDaddy, Namecheap, or similar registrars. Always purchase the privacy option to keep your personal information out of public records.

## Setting Up Professional Email

Create a professional email address using your domain: info@yourbusiness.com or yourname@yourbusiness.com. Avoid using Gmail, Yahoo, or Hotmail addresses for business communications. Google Workspace (around $6/month) provides reliable, professional email hosting using your custom domain.

## Securing Social Media Accounts

Even if you do not plan to use every platform immediately, secure your business name on all major social media platforms. This prevents others from claiming your name and gives you options for future expansion.

> **Tip:** Keep all passwords in a secure, organized system — a password manager or at minimum a password-protected spreadsheet. Use unique, auto-generated passwords for every account.

## Forming Your LLC

For most drone businesses, forming an LLC is the recommended approach. The key steps are: designate a registered agent (usually yourself), file articles of organization with your state (typically around $100), write an operating agreement defining your business purpose, ownership structure, and tax election, complete any local business license requirements, and apply for an EIN at irs.gov (free, and only use the official IRS website).

> **Tip:** If you are not comfortable doing this yourself, LegalZoom and similar services can handle LLC formation for around $100 plus state fees. It is a worthwhile investment to ensure everything is done correctly.

## Key Takeaways

- Research your business name thoroughly before committing — check trademarks, state records, domains, and social media
- Buy your domain and secure social media accounts before filing business paperwork
- Set up professional email using your domain name
- Form your LLC, get your EIN, and complete local business licensing
- Keep all account credentials organized and secure
