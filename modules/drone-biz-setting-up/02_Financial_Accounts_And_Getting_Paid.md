# Financial Accounts and Getting Paid

With your legal entity established, you need to set up the financial infrastructure to receive and manage money professionally.

## Business Bank Account

Open a dedicated business bank account that is completely separate from your personal finances. Shop around for a bank with no or low fees for small businesses. This separation is critical for maintaining your LLC's liability protection — if your business and personal finances are intermingled, a judge could disregard your LLC's protections.

## Business Credit Card

A business credit card helps track expenses and build business credit. Look for cards with cashback rewards since you will be spending on fuel, equipment, software, and marketing. Be disciplined — pay off the balance monthly and do not accumulate debt.

## Online Payment Processing

Set up at least one online payment system so customers can pay you electronically. The main options are PayPal, Stripe, and Square. Creating accounts is free; each charges approximately 3% per transaction. Research fee structures — some charge flat fees, others percentage-based. For in-person payments, Square and PayPal offer free card readers that plug into your phone.

## Getting Paid by Clients

You have several options for collecting payment:

**Paper/PDF invoices with check payment** — No processing fees, but slower. Clients may take days or weeks to process and mail checks.

**Electronic invoices through accounting software** — Tools like Xero or QuickBooks can send invoices and accept online payment. Payments are faster, but you pay processing fees. The advantage is automatic tracking of accounts receivable.

**In-person card processing** — Using Square or similar services for immediate payment at the job site.

> **Tip:** Build credit card processing fees (approximately 3%) into your pricing rather than charging extra for card payments. If a client pays by check, you come out 3% ahead.

## Tracking Your Finances

Use accounting software from the start. Xero and QuickBooks Online are the most popular options. Link your bank accounts and credit cards, set up your chart of accounts, and categorize every transaction. After the initial setup, much of this becomes automated. These tools generate your P&L, cash flow statements, and tax documents with minimal effort.

## Key Takeaways

- Keep business and personal finances completely separate
- Set up online payment processing to make it easy for clients to pay you
- Build processing fees into your prices rather than adding surcharges
- Use accounting software from day one — it saves enormous time and effort
