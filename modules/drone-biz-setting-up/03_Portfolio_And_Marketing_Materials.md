# Portfolio, Branding, and Marketing Materials

Before building your website and launching your marketing, you need professional materials that represent your brand consistently.

## Creating Your Portfolio or Demo Reel

Your portfolio is your most powerful sales tool. For photography and videography, showcase your best work across a variety of settings, lighting conditions, and project types. Create a demo reel (a short, polished video highlighting your capabilities) and a PDF portfolio for in-person presentations. For inspections or mapping, document sample projects showing both your process and deliverables.

> **Important:** Clients care about results, not equipment. Focus your portfolio on the quality of your deliverables, not on showcasing your drone. Show them what they will receive when they hire you.

If you do not yet have client work to showcase, create sample projects. Fly over interesting properties, capture footage at local landmarks, or volunteer to help a friend's business. Build your portfolio before you need it.

## Defining Your Marketing Look

Before creating any materials, decide on your visual identity: choose your brand colors (one or two primary colors), select fonts that match your positioning (modern, professional, creative), and study competitors and other businesses you admire for inspiration. Your visual identity should be consistent across everything — website, business cards, social media, vehicle wraps, and clothing.

## Creating Marketing Materials

If design is not your strength, hire a professional. The investment in quality design pays for itself in client perception. At minimum, you need a professional logo, business cards, and social media banners and profile images. Optional but valuable: brochures or flyers, vehicle graphics, branded clothing, and a tasteful watermark for your work.

> **Tip:** When you hire a designer, ask them to create a brand guide — a document showing your colors, fonts, logo usage rules, and design elements. This ensures consistency as you create future materials.

## Key Takeaways

- Your portfolio is your most powerful sales tool — invest in making it excellent
- Define your visual identity before creating any marketing materials
- Hire a professional designer if design is not your strength
- Maintain brand consistency across all touchpoints
