# Building Your Online Presence

Your online presence is the foundation of your visibility in the modern business landscape. This lesson covers your website, business listings, social media setup, reviews, and mailing list infrastructure.

## Your Website

Your website is the most important piece of online visibility you have. It must be visually appealing, provide clear information about your services and pricing, showcase your work effectively, include clear calls to action (contact forms, phone numbers, booking options), be mobile-friendly, and be findable through search engines.

Do not cut corners on your website. A $500 website might look decent but will likely be invisible to search engines. Invest in quality web design that includes SEO (Search Engine Optimization) from the start.

Use a CMS (Content Management System) like WordPress so you can update your own content without paying a developer for every change. Make sure you have access to edit your own site — never be locked into depending on a single designer.

## Understanding SEO

Search Engine Optimization is how your website gets found organically (without paid ads) on Google. It takes time — six months to a year to see significant results — but it provides long-term, free traffic. SEO requires substantial, relevant content on your site, proper technical structure, and consistent updates over time. Tools like SEMrush can help you research keywords and analyze competitors' SEO strategies.

> **Tip:** Do not only concentrate on how your website looks. A beautiful website that nobody can find is useless. Invest equally in visibility.

## Choosing Web Hosting

Your web host stores your website files and makes them accessible to visitors. Avoid the cheapest options — slow or unreliable hosting drives visitors away. Budget approximately $20-30 per month for quality hosting.

## Business Listings

Create profiles on Google Business Profile (essential for local search visibility) and Yelp. Keep your information current — hours, contact details, services offered. These listings significantly impact how Google perceives and ranks your business.

## Getting Reviews

Reviews build trust with potential customers. Ask satisfied clients to leave reviews on Google, Facebook, and Yelp. Respond to every review — positive and negative — professionally. Bad reviews happen to everyone. Respond thoughtfully, thinking about how future customers will perceive your response. Never be defensive; address concerns professionally and offer solutions.

## Setting Up Your Mailing List

Choose an email marketing provider (MailChimp, Constant Contact) and begin building your infrastructure. Create your automated email sequences, develop gated content to attract subscribers, and start collecting email addresses through your website.

## Sharing Your Work

Set up a system for delivering files to clients. Cloud-based solutions like Dropbox or Google Drive are recommended — they are easy to manage, allow you to control access, and work well for large files. Be aware that delivering work on physical media (USB drives, DVDs) may trigger sales tax requirements in some states.

## Key Takeaways

- Your website is your most important online asset — invest in quality and SEO
- Use a CMS so you can update your own content
- Create and maintain Google Business Profile and Yelp listings
- Actively collect and respond to customer reviews
- Set up your mailing list infrastructure and gated content early
- Use cloud-based file sharing for client deliverables
