# Identifying Your Target Customers

"Anyone who needs a drone" is not a target customer. It is a wish. The fastest way to waste a marketing budget is to advertise to everyone — your message becomes generic, your portfolio becomes scattered, and your pricing becomes confused. Pick a customer first, and the rest of your marketing falls into place.

## Why Specificity Wins

A generalist drone operator competes with every other generalist on price. A specialist — "I do roof inspections for property managers in the Twin Cities" — competes with almost no one. Specialisation makes your marketing cheaper, your sales conversations shorter, and your referrals far more useful.

> **Warning:** Specialising does not mean turning down work. It means choosing where to *aim* your marketing. You can still take a wedding gig that lands in your inbox while focusing your outreach on real estate.

## The Customer Profile

For each customer type you want to target, write down:

- **Who they are** — job title, industry, company size, location
- **What they need** — the actual outcome, not the deliverable. A realtor does not want "drone photos." They want listings that sell faster.
- **Where they spend time** — both online (LinkedIn? Instagram? trade publications?) and offline (associations, conferences, local groups)
- **What they value** — speed, price, polish, compliance, reliability
- **What they fear** — wasted money, legal liability, looking unprofessional, missing deadlines

This profile is the foundation of every marketing decision you will make. If you cannot answer all five points, you do not yet know your customer well enough.

**Consumer work** has more buyers, smaller budgets, and shorter sales cycles. **Commercial work** has fewer buyers, larger budgets, and longer sales cycles — but the clients are stickier and the marketing is more rational. Most successful drone businesses pick one and lean into it for the first two years.

## Talking to Real People

The customer profile is a guess until you have spoken with five real customers. Pick up the phone. Take five potential clients to coffee. Ask what they currently do, what frustrates them, and what would make a drone service worth paying for. You will learn more in five conversations than in fifty hours of internet research.

> **Tip:** When you call a prospect, lead with curiosity, not a pitch. "I am building a drone service for property managers and I want to make sure I am solving a real problem — can I ask you a few questions?" This works astonishingly well.

## Key Takeaways

- Specialising makes marketing cheaper, sales easier, and referrals more useful
- Build a customer profile covering who they are, what they need, where they are, what they value, and what they fear
- Most drone businesses should pick consumer/event work or commercial/technical work and focus there
- Five real conversations beat fifty hours of guessing
