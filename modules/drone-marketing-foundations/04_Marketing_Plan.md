# Building a Simple Marketing Plan

A marketing plan is not a 40-page document. It is a one-page system that tells you what to do this week, this month, and this quarter — and lets you measure whether it is working. The best marketing plan is the one you will actually follow.

## The One-Page Plan

Every drone business should be able to fit its marketing on a single page covering five things:

- **Audience** — who you are marketing to
- **Promise** — your value proposition
- **Channels** — where you will show up (one or two, not five)
- **Cadence** — what you will do, how often
- **Metrics** — how you will know it is working

If you cannot fit it on one page, the plan is too complicated to execute.

## Pick Two Channels, Not Five

The most common mistake is starting on too many channels at once. Each channel demands time, and most drone operators have one or two hours per week for marketing — not ten. Pick two channels that match where your customers actually are.

> **Warning:** "I will post on Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, YouTube, Facebook, and start a newsletter" is not a marketing plan. It is a recipe for inconsistency, which is the worst marketing outcome of all.

A useful pairing rule: one channel for **discovery** (where new customers find you) and one channel for **trust** (where you deepen relationships with people who already know you).

## Cadence: What and How Often

Cadence beats intensity. Two posts a week for a year crushes ten posts in one frantic month followed by silence. A typical sustainable cadence for a solo drone operator looks like:

- Two social posts per week on the discovery channel
- One short newsletter or update per month on the trust channel
- One outreach email per week to a warm prospect or past client
- One case study or portfolio update per quarter

That is roughly two hours a week — enough to build real momentum without burning out.

## Metrics That Matter

Marketing is full of vanity metrics. Likes, follower counts, and impressions feel good but rarely indicate revenue. Track instead:

- **Inquiries per month** — people reaching out, by channel
- **Conversion rate** — inquiries that turn into paying jobs
- **Cost per inquiry** — total marketing spend ÷ inquiries that month
- **Repeat / referral rate** — share of revenue from past clients or their referrals

> **Tip:** If a channel produces inquiries, double down. If it produces only likes, cut it. Be ruthless — your time is the scarcest resource in your business.

## Reviewing the Plan

Re-read your one-page plan on the first of every month. Ask: did I follow it? Did it produce inquiries? What is one thing I would change? A monthly check-in is the difference between a marketing plan and a marketing pile.

## Assignment 4

Write your one-page marketing plan covering audience, promise, channels, cadence, and metrics. Print it and tape it next to your editing workstation. Set a recurring calendar event on the first of each month titled "Marketing review" — fifteen minutes, never skipped.

## Key Takeaways

- A real marketing plan fits on one page — audience, promise, channels, cadence, metrics
- Pick two channels: one for discovery, one for trust. Skip the rest for now.
- Cadence beats intensity — small, consistent action wins
- Track inquiries and conversions, not likes and followers
- Review the plan monthly without exception
