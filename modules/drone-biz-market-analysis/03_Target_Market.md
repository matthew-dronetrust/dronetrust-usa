# Understanding Your Target Market

Now that you have identified your market segments, you need to understand your target customers in depth. The better you know them, the more effectively you can reach them and serve them.

## Demographics and Firmographics

For individual customers, consider their age, gender, occupation, income level, and education. For business customers, think about the company size, number of employees, industry, and annual revenue.

Most importantly: who is the decision-maker? If you are selling to a construction company, are you pitching to the CEO, the project manager, or a receptionist? Reaching the right person within an organization is critical. If you are talking to someone with no purchasing authority, you are wasting both your time and theirs.

## Geographic Considerations

Where are your customers located? Can you reach them efficiently, or will you need to travel extensively? Consider whether you can easily advertise in their area and whether the local economy supports the pricing you need to charge.

> **Tip:** If you live in a smaller market, you may need to travel to larger cities for enough business. Factor travel time and expenses into your pricing from the start.

## Customer Behavior

Understanding how your customers make purchasing decisions is crucial for your marketing strategy. Ask yourself these questions: Are your customers impulse buyers, or do they research extensively before purchasing? The higher your price point, the longer the decision process typically takes. Are they educated about drone services, or will you need to explain the value? Sometimes you have to create the demand by educating customers about what drones can do for them, much like Apple created demand for the iPhone before anyone knew they needed one. Where does their budget come from? A large corporation with a dedicated marketing budget operates very differently from a solo realtor paying out of pocket.

## Customer Loyalty

It is significantly more expensive to acquire a new customer than to retain an existing one. From the very beginning, think about how you will build relationships that generate repeat business. Deliver exceptional quality, be responsive and professional, and make your customers feel valued. If they love your work, they will come back — and they will refer you to others.

## Key Takeaways

- Understand your target customers deeply — demographics, location, behavior, and decision-making process
- Make sure you are reaching the actual decision-makers within organizations
- Factor geography and travel into your business model
- Plan for customer loyalty from day one — retention is cheaper than acquisition
