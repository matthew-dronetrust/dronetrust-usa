# Market Segmentation and TAM, SAM, SOM

Your market is not "everyone." Thinking it is will lead you to waste money on unfocused marketing and miss the customers who would actually pay for your services. You need to divide your potential market into smaller, more specific groups.

## Understanding Market Segmentation

Market segmentation means identifying specific groups of customers who share characteristics that make them likely to buy your service. For drone businesses, common ways to segment include whether you are selling to companies or individuals, the size of the businesses you are targeting, the budget your target customers have available, and the specific industry or niche you serve.

### Real Estate Example

If you are targeting real estate, you might segment your market by property type: low-end residential, mid-level residential, high-end residential (million-dollar homes), commercial real estate, and land-only listings. Each segment has different needs, budgets, and expectations. High-end residential clients may pay premium prices for exceptional quality, while commercial clients may need different deliverables entirely.

You could also segment by business size: individual realtors, small agencies (up to five agents), medium agencies (five to ten agents), and large firms (regional or national).

## TAM, SAM, and SOM

These three terms help you move from the broad market down to a realistic target:

**TAM (Total Available Market)** is the entire universe of potential customers. If there are 400 realtors in your area, that is your TAM.

**SAM (Segmented Available Market)** is the subset you will actually target based on your segmentation criteria. Maybe 100 of those 400 realtors match your ideal customer profile — they sell mid-to-high-end properties and have the budget for aerial photography.

**SOM (Share of Market)** is the realistic portion of your SAM you can capture. If you can realistically land 10 of those 100 realtors as regular clients, that is your SOM — 2.5% of your total available market, but enough to keep you busy full-time.

> **Important:** Be realistic when estimating your SOM. You will not capture 100% of your target segment, especially as a new entrant competing against established providers.

## Finding the Right Market

The ideal market for you is the one that gives you the greatest chance of success. Consider where there is an unmet need, where customers have budget available, and where you can realistically compete. You do not have to pick only one segment — you can target two or three — but do not try to serve them all.

Your market strategy is a living document. You might start targeting mid-level residential photography and later move upmarket as your portfolio and reputation grow.

## Key Takeaways

- Not everyone is your customer — segment your market into specific, targetable groups
- TAM is everyone, SAM is who you will target, SOM is what you can realistically capture
- Start with realistic estimates and adjust as you gain experience
- Your market strategy should evolve over time as your business grows
