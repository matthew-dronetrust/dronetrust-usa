# Brand Voice and Visual Identity

Once your name and logo exist, the brand has to actually *sound* and *look* consistent everywhere it appears. This is the work most drone operators skip — and it is the work that separates a real brand from a hobby with letterhead.

## Brand Voice: How You Sound

Your brand voice is the personality of every word that comes out of your business — captions, emails, voicemails, contracts, even the way you say hello on a call. It should be deliberate, not accidental. A useful exercise is to pick three voice attributes and one anti-attribute:

- **We are** — confident, plain-spoken, warm
- **We are not** — corporate, salesy, jokey

Those four words become a filter. If a draft Instagram caption sounds corporate, rewrite it. If a follow-up email reads as too jokey, soften it. Over time the filter becomes instinct.

> **Tip:** Read your last five customer emails out loud. If three different "voices" come out, your brand voice is not yet consistent. Pick the one that sounds most like *you* on a good day and standardise on that.

## Voice in Practice

Consistency does not mean stiffness. The same voice can flex across contexts — slightly more casual on Instagram, slightly more formal in a contract — without changing personality. Examples of the *same* brand voice across surfaces:

- **Instagram caption:** *"Listing went live this morning. Drone shots dropped two days early — figured the agent could use them."*
- **Cold email:** *"I noticed you have three luxury listings on the market. Would a 60-second aerial reel for the next one be useful?"*
- **Invoice note:** *"Thanks for the trust — let me know when the next shoot is on the books."*

Confident, plain-spoken, warm — same brand, three places. That is what voice consistency feels like.

## Templates Save You

Build a small library of reusable templates: a thumbnail format for video posts, a caption opener, an email signature, an invoice header, a quote document. Templates are not lazy — they are how you stay consistent when you are tired.

> **Important:** Before each post, ask one question: "Does this look and sound like *us*?" If anything feels off, fix it before publishing. Brand drift happens one rushed post at a time.

## Style Guide in One Page

You do not need a 30-page brand book. A single-page reference document is enough for most drone businesses, and you should make one:

- Logo files and clear-space rules
- Primary and secondary colours with hex codes
- Heading font and body font
- Voice attributes (three positives, one anti)
- A signature LUT or colour-grade reference
- One do/don't example for each

Save it where you can find it, and share it with anyone you collaborate with.

## Assignment 7

Define your three voice attributes plus one anti-attribute. Pick two brand colours and a heading/body font pairing — Google Fonts is fine. Build your one-page style guide. Re-edit your most recent reel with a single LUT and post it; note whether it gets meaningfully different engagement.

## Key Takeaways

- Brand voice is the personality of every word your business produces — make it deliberate
- A consistent voice can flex across contexts without changing personality
- Minimum visual identity: logo lockups, colours, fonts, and a consistent video colour grade
- Templates protect consistency when you are tired or in a hurry
- Capture the rules in a one-page style guide and reference it before every publish
