# Choosing a Name and Logo

Your name and logo are the two assets every customer sees first. They are not the brand, but they are how the brand is most often introduced. The good news: you do not need to be a designer or a copywriter to get this right. You need to make a few clear decisions and avoid a few common traps.

## Choosing a Name That Will Age Well

A great drone business name is short, easy to spell, easy to say on the phone, and not painted into a corner. The most common naming mistakes are:

- **Too literal** — "Springfield Drone Photography LLC" describes today perfectly and ages poorly. What happens when you add inspections or mapping?
- **Too cute** — "FlyHigh Bro Aerials" is memorable for the wrong reason in a B2B sales meeting.
- **Too generic** — "Aerial Imaging Solutions" is invisible and impossible to trademark.
- **Initials only** — "RJP Aerial" tells the customer nothing.

A good test: imagine printing the name across the side of a work truck that drives past a commercial client's office. Would it earn a phone call, or be forgotten in five seconds?

> **Tip:** Pick a name that allows you to expand later. "Northshore Aerial" is far more flexible than "Northshore Real Estate Drones," even if real estate is what you sell today.

Most operators commit too early — falling in love with a name before checking if the .com is available, the trademark is clear, or whether anyone outside their head can pronounce it. Slow down at step three.

> **Warning:** Always check the USPTO trademark database and your state's business name registry before committing. A surprise trademark dispute six months in is a painful, expensive lesson.

## Logos: Simple, Versatile, Owned

A logo's job is to be *recognisable* and *legible* — at the size of a thumbnail and at the size of a vehicle wrap. That rules out fussy detail, intricate gradients, or aggressive perspective. The strongest drone-business logos tend to share three qualities: a clean wordmark, one strong shape or icon, and a tight colour palette of two or three colours.

Three workable paths to a logo:

- **Wordmark only** — your name in a chosen typeface, period. Hard to mess up, easy to use everywhere.
- **Wordmark + icon** — your name plus a small mark that scales down well.
- **Custom mark** — paid designer work. Worth it once you are sure of your name and direction.

Avoid stock drone silhouettes. Every drone business has tried that, and it makes you look like every drone business.

> **Tip:** Hire a professional designer when you can — it is among the highest-leverage spends in your first year. Budget $400–$1,500 for a small but proper brand kit including logo, palette, and a font choice. Cheap fiverr work usually costs you more in rebrand expense later.

## Owning Your Brand Assets

Whoever you hire, make sure the contract states clearly that you own the logo files, vector originals, and rights to all variations. You should leave the engagement with editable source files (typically `.ai` or `.svg`), full-resolution exports, and a one-page brand sheet listing colours, fonts, and basic usage rules.

## Assignment 6

Brainstorm 30 candidate names for your business. Cut to 10 by speaking each out loud. Check `.com` availability, trademark status, and Instagram handles for the top 5. Pick three to test on real prospects and listen carefully to which one they remember a day later.

## Key Takeaways

- A great name is short, easy to say, future-proof, and not too literal or cute
- Always check domains, socials, and trademarks before committing
- A logo's job is recognisability and legibility — keep it simple and versatile
- Pay a real designer when you can, and own all source files in writing
