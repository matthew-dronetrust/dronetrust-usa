# Insurance and Protecting Yourself

Insurance is not optional for a drone business. One incident without coverage could destroy your business and personal finances.

## Types of Insurance

**General Liability** covers bodily injury, property damage, medical expenses, and lawsuits from general business operations.

**Professional Liability** covers negligence, errors, and mistakes during your professional drone work. Because drones are aviation, this is often provided by aviation-specialized insurers.

**Commercial Property** covers your business location against storms, fire, theft, and other hazards.

**Home-Based Business** protects equipment and provides liability coverage if you operate from home. Consider the scenario where a lithium battery fire destroys your home office and all equipment.

> **Tip:** Many insurers offer bundles that combine coverage types at a discount. But always compare bundled vs. separate policies.

## Full-Time vs. Per-Job Insurance

Starting out, per-job insurance (like Skywatch at skywatch.ai) may be more cost-effective. As volume increases, an annual policy becomes cheaper. Calculate your break-even point.

## Insurance Providers to Research

Skywatch (hourly/monthly/annual), Global Aerospace, Harpino Insurance, Aviation Specialty Insurance, and Hull and Husher (photography-focused). Always shop around and get multiple quotes.

## Pro Tips

Sit down with your insurance underwriter and explain your operations. Share your standard operating procedures. Companies that demonstrate safety awareness often receive lower rates. Reassess your rates every year — do not auto-renew without checking competitors.

## Location

If you plan to have an office, consider the cost (rent, utilities), visibility to customers, and the length of your lease commitment. If competitors succeed without one, maybe you can too. Many drone businesses start from home to keep overhead low.

## Key Takeaways

- Insurance is mandatory — general liability and professional liability at minimum
- Per-job insurance works for part-timers; annual policies for full-time operators
- Talk to your insurer, share safety procedures, and shop around annually
- Keep overhead low — start from home if possible
